# Frontier review (2026-11-09)

This review looked at current work near FinScope's job: turning official filings into answers a reader can check. Each candidate is judged by one question: would a visitor or a reviewer notice the difference, and can the change be verified in this release? The review covers eight candidates, with primary sources and dates. It was written for the 0.2.0 iteration ("Ask the ledger"); see [CHANGELOG.md](../CHANGELOG.md).

## 1. Grounded numeric financial QA

**What the field shows.** FinQA ([Chen et al., EMNLP 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00122)) and TAT-QA ([Zhu et al., ACL 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.07624)) framed financial questions as a program over table and text values: the answer is the result of explicit operations on cited numbers, and models were scored on both the program and the result. FinanceBench ([Islam et al., November 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11944)) asked open questions about real 10-K/10-Q filings and found that retrieval-augmented language models answered many questions incorrectly or refused, with errors that a reader could not easily detect. DocFinQA ([Reddy et al., ACL 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06915)) moved FinQA to full-length filings and found that long context made the task markedly harder. FinTagging ([May 2025, revised January 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20650)) benchmarks the step underneath: linking reported numbers to US-GAAP concepts. Models found the numbers but struggled to choose the right fine-grained concept.

**What it implies here.** FinScope already has the hard part done by the filer: its facts are XBRL-tagged, period-matched and linked to their filing. The failure mode these benchmarks document is choosing the wrong number or concept, then stating it fluently. A typed-question tier should therefore (a) map the question onto a closed set of concepts and periods, (b) let the program fetch the tagged facts and do the arithmetic, and (c) refuse when the question names a concept the bundle does not hold, instead of substituting a near neighbour (gross margin for operating margin, debt for total liabilities, investing for operating cash flow).

**Adopted.** `web/ask-engine.js` reads the question with a closed vocabulary (English and Chinese, simplified and common traditional characters), produces a plan (companies, figure, period, topic) and answers from `research.json`. Every number comes from a fact or from a stated formula over facts. Look-alikes the bundle does not hold are refused by name. The plan, not free text, is what the blind evaluation scores.

## 2. Citation discipline

**Source.** ALCE ([Gao et al., EMNLP 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627)) measures whether each generated statement is supported by the cited passage, and found that strong models still left a large share of statements without full support.

**Adopted.** FinScope's answers are generated by the program, so support can be enforced rather than measured. Each stated figure is a fact row with form, XBRL tag, period, filing date, accession and a link to the matched primary document. A test runs every coverage question and fails if an answer states a figure without a source line, if a non-answer states any figure, or if a link is not on `www.sec.gov` or `home.treasury.gov`.

## 3. Earnings-quality indicators

**Sources.** Sloan ([The Accounting Review 71(3), 1996](https://www.jstor.org/stable/248290)) showed that the accrual component of earnings is less persistent than the cash component. Hribar and Collins ([Journal of Accounting Research 40(1), 2002](https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-679X.00041)) showed that accruals measured from the cash-flow statement avoid the errors of the balance-sheet approach. Beneish ([Financial Analysts Journal 55(5), 1999](https://doi.org/10.2469/faj.v55.n5.2296)) estimated an eight-index model on firms later found to have manipulated earnings. Dechow, Ge, Larson and Sloan ([Contemporary Accounting Research 28(1), 2011](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1911-3846.2010.01041.x)) built the F-score from SEC enforcement releases.

**Adopted.** The bundle's accrual ratio is already the cash-flow-statement form, (net income − operating cash flow) ÷ average total assets. This release surfaces it in answers with its inputs, and adds two indicators that need no new data: cash conversion (operating cash flow ÷ net income) and free cash flow (operating cash flow − cash capital expenditure). Each answer shows the formula and the facts used, including the prior-year assets behind the average. A test recomputes the accrual ratio from the cited facts and requires it to equal the pipeline value.

**Skipped: M-score and F-score.** Both need inputs the bundle does not hold: receivables, cost of revenue, property and equipment, SG&A, depreciation and debt for the M-score; receivables, inventory, soft assets, cash sales and share issuance for the F-score. Adding them means changing the scheduled refresh pipeline and its validation. Both were estimated on firms with known misstatements and output a probability-like score. On six mega-cap platform companies, a reader would take that score as a fraud verdict, which the product explicitly does not give. The Ask box refuses fraud questions and points to the transparent indicators instead.

## 4. ECOD and newer unsupervised detectors

**Sources.** ECOD ([Li et al., IEEE TKDE, arXiv January 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.00382)) scores points by per-feature empirical tail probabilities, with no parameters to tune. ADBench ([Han et al., NeurIPS 2022 Datasets and Benchmarks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.09426)) compared 30 detectors on 57 datasets and found no method that is best in general. The Isolation Forest ([Liu, Ting and Zhou, ICDM 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDM.2008.17)) remains a standard baseline.

**Skipped.** There are no labelled outcomes for these company-years, so no held-out comparison can say which detector is better at anything that matters. A switch chosen by eye would only change which profiles look unusual. ECOD's per-feature tails would be easier to explain, which is attractive, but the current percentile already comes with the three ratios furthest from the training medians. What a future comparison would need is written down so that it can be pre-registered: freeze both detectors' settings; train on company-years ending before a cutoff; on the later held-out years, compare rank stability under small input perturbations and agreement with a reviewer's blind list of profiles worth reading. Adopt only if one is better on both.

## 5. XBRL data-quality rules

**Source.** The XBRL US Data Quality Committee publishes freely licensed validation rules for SEC filings ([rule list](https://xbrl.us/data-rule/)), for example [DQC_0015](https://xbrl.us/data-rule/dqc_0015-v2) (elements that should not be negative; last updated June 2024 in version 24).

**Skipped this iteration.** DQC rules run on complete instance documents with their calculation and dimension structure. The bundle keeps about ten selected facts per period, so most rules have nothing to test. The useful subset is small: non-negative revenue, assets and cash; current assets ≤ total assets; cash ≤ current assets; current liabilities ≤ total liabilities. These belong in the Python validation that gates the scheduled refresh (`scripts/build_research.py`), not in the page, and that pipeline also writes the bot-refreshed data on `main`. The checks are recorded here as the next pipeline change.

## 6. Live SEC data in the browser, Pyodide, and visitors' own companies

**CORS.** A browser page on another origin can read a response only if the server sends `Access-Control-Allow-Origin`. The repository's `SEC CORS probe` workflow (`.github/workflows/sec-cors-probe.yml`) requested CompanyFacts, Submissions and the company-ticker list with a browser `Origin` header and a CORS preflight, using the repository's declared SEC user agent. Result on 2026-11-09 ([run 42621395684](https://github.com/leon1111-wgl/finscope/actions/runs/42621395684)):

| Endpoint | GET with an `Origin` header | Preflight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/…` (the figures) | 200, **no** `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` | 403 |
| `data.sec.gov/submissions/…` (filing metadata) | 200, `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` | 200 |
| `www.sec.gov/files/company_tickers.json` | 200, **no** `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` | 403 |

So a page could read filing metadata but not the financial facts. The figures still have to come through a server-side job, as FinScope's scheduled refresh does today. One request per endpoint is a snapshot of SEC's current configuration, not a documented guarantee. SEC's [fair-access policy](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data) also asks automated clients to declare a user agent with contact details, which a browser page cannot do for its visitors.

**Pyodide.** Pyodide ([0.29 documentation](https://pyodide.org/en/0.29.3/usage/packages-in-pyodide.html)) can run the existing Python validation in the browser. It would be a download of several megabytes for the runtime alone, plus pandas (about 4 MB) and scikit-learn (about 6 MB) wheels ([pyodide channel listing](https://anaconda.org/channels/pyodide/packages/scikit-learn/files)). The tier-1 rule is an instant answer with no large download, so Pyodide does not fit that tier.

**Skipped: a visitor's own company.** Because CompanyFacts is not readable cross-origin, a visitor would have to download it from SEC by hand and upload it; the filing links could then be fetched from Submissions. Doing this correctly means porting the annual and year-to-date period matching, restatement selection and filing-link checks to JavaScript with parity tests against the Python pipeline, plus a decision on the anomaly score (which needs the training sample). That cannot be done completely and checked within this iteration, so it is left out rather than shipped half done. Questions about other companies get a clear "not in this bundle" with the SEC EDGAR company search.

## 7. A browser language model

Other projects in this portfolio tried a small open-weights model in the browser. In LoopDesk a real WebGPU run failed after about eleven minutes on a CI runner, and could have gained at most one of 150 blind cases. FinScope's answers are numbers that the program must state with their source line; a model could at most help read unfamiliar wording. There is no measurement suggesting it would beat the closed vocabulary on that, and it would add a 350 MB download. Not built.

## 8. Time-series foundation models for the forecasting lab

TimesFM 2.5 ([Google Research, September 2025](https://github.com/google-research/timesfm)) and Chronos-2 ([Amazon, arXiv October 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15821)) are open forecasting models that perform well on public benchmarks. The forecasting lab is a separate, synthetic volatility study with five pre-registered model variants and transaction-cost sensitivity. Adding a foundation model would need runner-side inference, a new pre-registered comparison and new synthetic data, and a visitor would not see the difference. Forecasting real companies would also contradict the product's "no forecasts" rule. Skipped.
